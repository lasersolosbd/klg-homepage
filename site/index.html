Three questions your board is about to ask.

You don’t need an AI strategy deck. You need straight answers, and a small number of things that work by next quarter.

  • “What are we doing about AI?”

    Staff are already pasting donor emails into free tools. A short, plain-language policy turns that from a risk into a decision your board can make once and stand behind.

  • “Why doesn’t ChatGPT mention us?”

    Donors now ask AI assistants who to give to. If your organization isn’t part of the answer, you’re invisible at the moment someone is ready to act. That’s fixable, and measurable.

  • “Can we do more with the team we have?”

    Most of the hours that disappear every week are the same hours: copying between systems, chasing reports, returning the same calls. Software should take those, so people can take the donor lunch.

Questions leaders ask first.

We don’t have anyone technical. Is that a problem?

No. We build and maintain the systems. You need one person who can explain how your work really flows and approve what we make.

What happens with our donor data?

Before anything is built, we write down exactly which data a project touches. If a project doesn’t need donor records, it never sees them.

Will this replace our staff?

No. The goal is more reach for a small team. Every system keeps a person in the loop, and nothing goes out under your name without your review.

Is our organization the right size?

We’re built for nonprofits raising roughly $1M to $30M a year: big enough to feel the workload, lean enough that every hour counts.

What does it cost?

Engagements are scoped to the project. The first conversation is where you get a clear range, before anything is signed.
